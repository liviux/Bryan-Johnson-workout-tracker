# The plan (v2, October 2026) — readable snapshot

> This is the plan the app is built on, as one document you can read on GitHub. The app is the live version: it personalises heart rates and weights for each user and tracks everything. Numbers here are for the profile the plan was first written for (male, 38, 188 cm, 75 kg); each level column shows the weights that level would suggest for him.

A six-day indoor training plan laid out like Bryan Johnson’s newest weekly template (October 2026): strength Monday, Wednesday and Friday, short intervals Tuesday, the Norwegian 4×4 Thursday and controlled vigorous cardio + Zone 2 Saturday. Rebuilt for bodyweight, adjustable dumbbells, a pull-up bar and resistance bands, with a daily posture block for tech neck and forward shoulders.

Made for: male, 38, 188 cm, 75 kg, trained before, four years off, no injuries.

- All sessions at home; Sunday rest
- Monday–Saturday: 36–55 min per session at New to training, 47–71 min at Returning, 65–82 min at Active, 72–93 min at Well trained
- Plus a posture block every day: ≈8 min, ≈10 min on Wednesday and Saturday
- Estimated max heart rate 182 bpm (Zone 2 = 109–127 bpm)

Each movement has its own row. Dynamic warm-ups run once through. Paired strength exercises alternate one set each; Friday's Well trained deadlifts alternate with 3 jumps after a 20–30 second wait. Interval timers show each movement and recovery in round order.

Each table lists the dose for each training level. A dash means the exercise is skipped at that level. Click an exercise name for how-to steps, the minute where Bryan does it, and demo videos.

The app’s source timeline and source lists show supporting links with separate dates, newest first where known. This presentation does not change the training doses below.

## Training levels

Your training level alone decides the workouts; nothing moves on by itself. Change it in the app's Settings when you feel ready for harder exercises or want easier ones. Days you already logged keep the level they were done at.

### New to training

_Little or no strength training before._

**Effort 4–5 out of 10: stop each set with 5 or more easy reps left.**

- 1–2 sets, with supported or smaller-range versions (a hand on a chair or wall). Learn the positions first.
- No jumps, swings, power skips or Nordics yet; hops stay tiny.
- Tuesday: 4 brisk 20 s bursts. Thursday: 2 rounds of 2 min brisk, well below all-out.
- 60 min of scheduled Zone 2 a week across five days. Intervals stay brisk, below vigorous effort. Mild muscle soreness is normal; joint pain is not.

_Move up to Returning after about 3 weeks (around 18 sessions) when every session feels comfortable and nothing hurts the next day._

### Returning

_Trained before, but out of practice for months or years._

**Effort 5–6 out of 10: stop each set with about 4 good reps left.**

- 2 sets on main lifts, 1 set on Bryan’s high-rep accessories. Relearn the positions.
- Jumps are landing drills only (snap-downs). Nordics are slow lowers only.
- Tuesday: 20 s brisk bursts with long recoveries. Thursday: 2 rounds of 3 min, then 3, controlled.
- 90 min of scheduled Zone 2 and about 26 min of vigorous work a week. Some muscle soreness is expected; joint pain is not. Still sore after 48 hours? Repeat sessions before moving up.

_Move up to Active after about 2 weeks (around 12 sessions) when you reach the top of every rep range with clean form and soreness is gone within a day._

### Active

_Training regularly over the last few months._

**Effort about 7 out of 10: 2–3 reps left in each set.**

- 3 sets on most strength work. Real jumps start; stick every landing.
- Tuesday: 8 × 20 s hard. Thursday: 3 rounds of 4 min, then 4.
- At least 120 min of scheduled Zone 2 and about 51 min of hard cardio a week; controlled vigorous blocks on Tue/Thu/Sat.

_Move up to Well trained after about 4 weeks (around 24 sessions) when 4 rounds of the 4×4 stay controlled and your weights keep going up._

### Well trained

_Lifting and doing hard cardio consistently for a year or more._

**Effort 8 out of 10 on the main lifts (1–2 reps left); Bryan’s 1 × 25-style accessories go close to fatigue.**

- Galpin-style heavy work on the big lifts, with jump contrast after Friday’s RDL.
- Tuesday: two blocks of 8 × 20 s all-out. Thursday: the full Norwegian 4×4 at 155–173 bpm.
- 150 min of scheduled Zone 2 and about 75 min of hard cardio a week, including 18 controlled vigorous minutes on Tue/Thu/Sat. A weighted vest is optional for Zone 2.

_This is the full plan. Keep adding weight with the rules below; after illness, injury or a long break, step down a level for a while._

### Rules

- Add weight only when you hit the top of the rep range on every set with clean form, two sessions in a row: about +1–2 kg per dumbbell for upper body, +2–4 kg for legs. Bryan’s protocol calls progressive overload the key, raised gradually.
- Injury prevention is Bryan’s priority #1. Swap to the easier variant (open the exercise) whenever a joint complains.
- The 4×4 is hard but controlled. If you can’t hold the pace in the last round, start slower next time.
- Rough week (bad sleep, stress, sickness): repeat sessions or step down a level for a while instead of pushing on.
- After four inactive years, get a basic check-up (blood pressure, resting ECG) before your first all-out intervals.

- Met: Balance or stability on 5 days, stretching every day, posture every day with his two physical-therapist exercises to fatigue twice a week.

## Posture program

Built from Bryan’s posture guide (5 habits, updated March 2026) and the posture work in his routines, plus two classics you asked for (chin tucks and the doorway stretch), marked as not Bryan’s.

### Habits

1. **String cue.** Every time you sit down or stand up, imagine the string pulling the crown of your head up.
2. **Phone at eye level.** Bryan calls the phone the worst trigger for bad posture. Lift it to eye level instead of looking down; put the top of your monitor at eye level too.
3. **Move every 30 minutes.** Get up for 2–3 minutes every 30 minutes and rotate between sitting and standing, as Bryan does at his desk. Your under-desk treadmill is ideal for this.
4. **10 squats every ~45 minutes.** Bryan’s April 2026 tip for long sitting days, from a study where it beat one 30-minute walk for blood sugar. He stressed that breaking up the sitting is what matters.
5. **Two strength exercises, twice a week.** Shoulders up and out + neck forehead resistance, 1 set to fatigue each, on Wednesday and Saturday.
6. **Expect soreness.** Bryan’s guide: holding good posture takes muscle, and feeling sore at first is normal. Keep going. Sharp pain, tingling or headaches are different: stop and get it checked.

### How you’ll know you’re OK

- Every 2 weeks, take a relaxed side photo in the same spot. The ear should sit over the shoulder, not in front of it.
- Wall check: heels about 5 cm from a wall, buttocks and shoulder blades touching. The back of your head should reach the wall with your eyes level, without straining.
- Each evening, rate neck and upper-back stiffness from 0 to 10.
- When the photo and wall check look right and stiffness stays at 2 or below for 4 weeks, switch to maintenance: keep the two strength exercises twice a week and the habits (that is what Bryan keeps doing), and cut the daily block to 2–3 times a week.

#### Heels-elevated goblet squat (slant-board squat)

> **From Bryan:** Squats open Day 1 of Bryan’s official protocol (3 × 10–15). His own version is on a slant board: 3 × 10–15 in 2023, a slant-board goblet squat for 2 × 5–8 in his 2025 Galpin session.

#### Poliquin step-up

> **From Bryan:** A Bryan staple (3 × 10 per leg) learned from Ben Patrick, the Knees-Over-Toes coach now behind his 2026 dunk project. Builds the inner quad (VMO) and knee resilience.

On Wednesday at New to training, 2 × 3 band-assisted pull-ups are an optional replacement for the prescribed dead hang. Choose one.

1. Grip the bar with your palms facing away, slightly wider than shoulder-width.
2. Pull your chest toward the bar, then lower over 2–3 seconds to a full hang.
3. Use a band or controlled negatives when prescribed.

#### Chin-up (palms facing you)

1. Grip the bar with your palms facing you, shoulder-width apart.

#### Push-ups

> **From Bryan:** Day 1 of Bryan’s official protocol and his newest template: 3 × 8–12.

1. Hands under the shoulders, body in one line (squeeze glutes and abs).
2. Elbows about 45° from the body.
3. Lower until the chest is a fist above the floor, then press to full lockout.
4. Too hard: hands on a sofa or bench. Too easy: 3 s down, or feet raised.

#### One-arm dumbbell row

> **From Bryan:** Day 1 of Bryan’s official protocol (3 × 10–12). In the Galpin session he does a harder version balancing on one leg; keep both feet down for now.

1. One hand and one knee on a chair or bed, back flat, dumbbell hanging.
2. Pull the dumbbell toward your hip, elbow close to the body.
3. Squeeze 1 s, lower to a full stretch.
4. No twisting of the torso.

#### Standing dumbbell overhead press

> **From Bryan:** In the Friday session of Bryan’s newest weekly template (exercise newsletter, 4 Oct 2026) and Day 5 of his official protocol (3 × 10–12).

#### Norwegian 4×4

> **From Bryan:** Thursday in Bryan’s newest weekly template (exercise newsletter, 4 Oct 2026): 4 rounds of 4 min at 85–95% of max heart rate with 3 min at 60–70% between, hard but controlled rather than an all-out sprint, starting with 2–3 rounds if four is too much. Bryan’s own variant (X, Dec 2024) is 4 min all-out with 4 min recovery.

1. 10 min easy warm-up that builds to about 130 bpm.
2. 4 min hard: reach 155–173 bpm and hold an even pace you could repeat four times.
3. 3 min easy: march or step slowly (109–127 bpm). Keep moving.
4. Repeat, then 5 min easy. Indoors, change the move every minute so the legs don’t fail before the heart and lungs.

- Tip: If round 4 falls apart, start slower next time. Don’t sprint the first minute.

#### Controlled vigorous cardio (indoors)

> **From Bryan:** Bryan targets at least 75 min of high-intensity cardio a week, with only about 10 min near maximum effort. This home block supplies controlled vigorous minutes alongside his Tuesday bursts and Thursday 4×4; its duration is a home-plan adaptation, not a session copied from his sample week.

1. Warm up first. Use continuous low-step step-ups, fast marching, high knees or step jacks, changing the move before local muscle fatigue spoils your form.
2. Follow your level’s heart-rate target, roughly 146–164 bpm at the higher levels. Breathing is hard and you can manage only a few words; keep an even pace rather than sprinting.
3. Log only the minutes at the prescribed effort. Pause or slow down when needed; easy recovery does not count as vigorous time.
4. Finish with the scheduled Zone 2 or cool-down. Use the quiet versions of the indoor interval moves when needed.

- Tip: This is controlled conditioning. Save near-max efforts for the short bursts and the ends of the 4×4 rounds.

Watch: [Low-impact indoor cardio moves](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gi7pZ8CYzvk)

#### Zone 2 cardio (indoors)

> **From Bryan:** Bryan’s protocol: at least 150 min a week of moderate activity (Zone 2) during which you can hold a conversation.

1. Heart rate 109–127 bpm (60–70% of max). You can speak in full sentences.
2. Main indoor option: continuous step-ups on a stable low aerobic step, switching the lead leg every minute. March if stepping raises your effort too much.
3. Mix in brisk marching with arm swings, side steps or easy jump rope to break the boredom.
4. Music, a podcast or a series helps; Bryan rotates music, books and podcasts too.

- Tip: When you get the chance, a brisk walk, hike or bike ride outside counts the same.

Watch: [Simple beginner step cardio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aAHCk7dbnKM) · [10-minute low-impact step workout](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A0dUuUTe4jQ)

3. Finish with thumbs pointing back and shoulder blades squeezed; hold 1 s.
4. Slow return; stay tall.

Watch: [Bryan: My Complete Anti-Aging Workout (Jul 2023, gym version) at ≈3:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYyVi-H-ozI&t=217s) · [Band face pull with a door anchor](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Q77ORURfeYc) · [Face pulls with a door anchor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fe37Ll4VDfI)

#### External rotation with elbow on knee

1. Sit with one foot on a stable chair and rest the working elbow on the inside of that knee, bent 90°.
2. Hold a light dumbbell and rotate the forearm upward without moving the elbow.
3. Lower over 2–3 seconds, keeping the shoulder blade back and down.

Watch: [Elbow-on-knee shoulder external rotation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mbdDt0c9bZY) · [Dumbbell external rotation with the elbow on the knee](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7hfjcXtXi0w&t=15s)

#### Side-lying external rotation

1. Lie on your side with the upper elbow bent 90° and held against your ribs.
2. Rotate a light dumbbell upward while keeping your elbow still.
3. Lower over 2–3 seconds without rolling your torso.

Watch: [Side-lying dumbbell external rotation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=brwBvldn4gw) · [Side-lying external rotation form tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3XLT7kpMzcM&t=12s)

#### Forehead resistance hold

1. Sit or stand tall with your chin slightly tucked.
2. Place a hand on your forehead and resist gentle backward pressure without moving your head.
3. Use 30–50% effort for the prescribed holds. Stop for dizziness, tingling or a headache.

Watch: [Isometric cervical flexion (forehead hold)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aF6txnLeC04) · [Isometric cervical flexion for neck strengthening](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4rK-m6GvNCk&t=8s)

#### Slow forehead resistance

1. Sit or stand tall with a long neck and your chin slightly tucked.
2. Press gently against your forehead and let your hand move your head backward slowly over 3–5 seconds, up to about 30°.
3. Return slowly and follow today's dose. Stop for dizziness, tingling or a headache.

Watch: [Controlled neck flexion against hand resistance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WnKcq1Uh8m4&t=5s)

#### Shoulders up and out (levator shrug)

> **From Bryan:** Bryan’s physical-therapist exercise #1. Official wording (2026): “With weights in your hands, bring your shoulders up and out. Keep your elbows back. Do 1 set to fatigue 2 times a week.” He uses about 9 kg (20 lb); his posture coach’s patients usually start near 3.5 kg (8 lb).

1. Stand tall with a light dumbbell in each hand, neck long, shoulders set back and down first.
2. Bend the elbows slightly and keep them pulled back, slightly behind the body.
3. Lift the shoulders up and out in a small arc, toward the ears and slightly forward.
4. Pause 1 s at the top, lower slowly. Learn it light: Bryan says it took him time to get the technique.

Watch: [Bryan: How I Fixed My Terrible Posture – 5 Habits at ≈2:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=in9ubCilsT8&t=158s) · [Bryan: My Complete Anti-Aging Workout (Jul 2023, gym version) at ≈2:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYyVi-H-ozI&t=140s) · [Bryan: A 20-Minute Call with My Posture Coach at ≈11:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cYGlj8Ryij8&t=703s)

#### Doorway chest stretch

> **Not Bryan’s (classic):** Not one of Bryan’s exercises. A standard stretch for tight chest muscles that pull the shoulders forward, added at your request.

1. Forearms on the door frame, elbows at shoulder height and bent 90°.
2. Step one foot through and lean gently until you feel the stretch across the chest.
3. Ribs down, no low-back arch, no pinching in the shoulder.

Watch: [Physical therapy tip: doorway pec stretch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=069o76BafTk) · [Doorway pec and shoulder stretch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dmm8_S23I74)

### Hanging and impact movements

#### Passive dead hang

1. Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder-width and let your body hang without swinging.
2. Relax your shoulders and breathe steadily for the prescribed time.
3. Support some of your weight with your feet when prescribed.

Watch: [Passive dead hang tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ny4AVinFBmI) · [Passive vs active hang key differences](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7DcNQ_bDaME) · [Build hanging from zero (beginner progression)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=no_ZdiJsLu4)

#### Active dead hang

1. Hang from the bar with straight elbows.
2. Draw your shoulder blades down and slightly back, away from your ears.
3. Hold without swinging and breathe steadily. Support your feet if needed.

Watch: [How to do an active hang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0_YZc2yuKkE) · [Active hang technique and scapular pull](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ny4AVinFBmI) · [Dead hang vs active hang explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GwMTIwfOPQE)

#### Heel drops

1. Stand tall with feet hip-width apart on a firm surface or thin mat.
2. Rise smoothly onto the balls of your feet, lifting heels 3–5 cm.
3. Drop your full body weight firmly down onto your heels with knees slightly soft (do not lock them).
4. Feel the mechanical vibration pulse up through the shins, femur neck and lumbar spine.
5. Perform rhythmic, controlled drops: 20–30 reps for beginners, up to 50 for trained.

Watch: [Heel drop exercise for bone density](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZ8gy_V6bxM) · [How to do heel drops for osteoporosis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d3FVrOVuQtM)

#### Stomp drills

1. Stand tall beside a wall if you need support.
2. Lift one foot to mid-shin height and place the whole foot down firmly, keeping the knee soft.
3. Alternate feet. Reduce the impact or stop if it causes pain.

Watch: [Foot stomp exercise for strengthening bone](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kMbajq1u-Us) · [Bone-building stomp drill](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hpg5COs6LNA)
